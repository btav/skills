# Prose checklist

Apply this checklist within the calling skill's task and output format. It does not activate the standalone rewrite workflow. For a specific pattern that needs clarification, consult the relevant section of the [trope reference](tropes.md); do not load the full catalog routinely.

- Preserve meaning, voice, register, and uncertainty. Do not invent facts, motivation, history, opinions, or first-person experience. Keep ambiguity when the supplied text does not resolve it.
- Preserve technical terms, proper nouns, numbers, code, and quoted material. Prefer plain words where they are equally precise; retain domain terms the reader needs.
- Put the point where the reader can find it. Cut repetition, filler, hedge stacks, vague hype, and summaries that add nothing. Keep qualifications that affect the claim.
- Describe what happens and who does it. Use concrete details already supplied; when empty rhetoric carries no fact, cut it rather than inventing a replacement. Passive voice is fine when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Keep sentences easy to parse and varied in length. Split dense clauses when that helps; keep clear short sentences when they fit. Do not chase a word count or sentence-length pattern.
- Use one name for each thing. Avoid cycling synonyms and unexplained labels that make the reader infer whether two names refer to the same thing.
- Remove repeated rhetorical hooks, forced contrasts, inflated stakes, and dramatic fragments when they obscure the point. Judge the pattern in context rather than banning an otherwise useful word or construction.
- Preserve the document's structure and formatting unless the task calls for restructuring. Avoid decorative formatting and repeated em dashes; do not introduce new em dashes, curly quotes, or unicode arrows.
- Make the smallest edit that improves the text. Cut padding without summarizing away useful content, and leave clean wording unchanged. Remove assistant preambles, sign-offs, and commentary from the finished artifact.
